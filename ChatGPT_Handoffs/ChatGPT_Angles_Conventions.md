I built the comparison, including **ID28, APS Sector 30 HERIX, and BL43LXU**, with TAVI alongside the neutron instruments.

- **Scattering_Angle_Conventions.xlsx** — five filterable tabs covering angle roles, sample and Greek names, frames and calibration, and a proposed common mounting model.
- **Scattering_Angle_Conventions.pdf** — an 11-page printable reference, including the source catalogue.

The useful common standard is **a physical mounting frame and an ordered set of axes, with each instrument’s names attached to those definitions**. The research found several cases where the same Greek letter describes different physical quantities.

**Reading the charts:** **NV** means *not verified in the recovered primary documentation*. **N/A** means the role does not apply to the stated instrument layout. Each row is scoped to particular instruments, software, and documentation eras.

## 1. Neutron TAS: the six physical roles

Here, “sample rotation” means the crystal’s orientation coordinate. It is generally independent of half the sample scattering angle. For monochromator and analyzer Bragg reflections, the usual θ–2θ relationship couples the angles; at fixed lattice spacing, they change when that optic’s selected wavelength changes. [ifit.mccode.org](http://ifit.mccode.org)

Two points answer the earlier questions directly:

- **MLZ matches ILL at the level of physical roles.** A numerical conversion needs more than renaming: the inspected PUMA setup enables sample-axis coupling, whereas PANDA retains the uncoupled default. Relative monochromator/analyzer readouts also differ between their configurations. These settings are detailed in the workbook.
- **I can verify the four-angle scheme in TAVI.** I could not substantiate it as a universal historical “Brookhaven A1–A4” standard from the primary material recovered. The 1975 Brookhaven source uses the qualified `C1/A1 … C3/A3` motor-number configuration above.

## 2. High-resolution IXS comparison

The essential distinction is between **the ray leaving the sample** and **the Bragg reflection inside an analyzer crystal**. Both involve angles, but they belong to different parts of the geometry.


| Beamline / documentation scope                                 | Monochromator and energy scan                                                                                                                                            | Sample rotations                                                                                                                                                                                                   | Sample scattering and analyzer angles                                                                                                                                                                                                                  |
| -------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **ESRF ID28 — documented 2014–2015 configuration**             | Main HR mono `mth`, with `mchi` alignment tilt; separate premono `mono`. Near-backscattering operation; energy scans change mono temperature.                            | Verified standard carrier order: `th → sphi` **(physical** `phi`**)** `→ chi → optional adth`. `th` is vertical; `sphi` is a transverse tilt; `chi` is beam-parallel at the zero outer-stage pose.                 | `tth` is an angular pseudomotor derived from linear `tthm`. Channel offsets specify individual outgoing rays. `ath_i/achi_i` align analyzer crystals. [esrf.fr](http://esrf.fr)                                                                        |
| **APS Sector 30 HERIX — 2011/2020 optics, 2025 stage listing** | **Angle-tuned, six-reflection in-line HRM.** Energy scans rotate its central crystal pair. It has several Bragg angles, rather than one TAS-like mono θ/2θ pair.         | Official stage names **θ, χ, Φ**. Drawings label base circles **θ1/θ2** and support the mechanical interpretation **θ1 → χ → Φ → sample carrier**. Exact controller aliases, signs and calibrated zeros remain NV. | Sample scattering is **2θ**; analyzers form a horizontal bank. Each near-backscattering analyzer has **θx pitch / θy yaw** alignment. Those adjustments are distinct from sample χ/Φ. [journals.iucr.org](http://journals.iucr.org)                    |
| **SPring-8 BL43LXU — high-resolution IXS, 2015–2025 sources**  | Near-backscattering HR mono; energy scans change its temperature while analyzer temperatures remain stable. Native mono motor aliases are NV in the recovered documents. | Official default circles **ω, χ, φ**, using a Huber 512 Eulerian cradle. The complete signed axis definitions and rotational nesting remain NV.                                                                    | Large **two-theta** arm; official SIXCIRCLE instructions use `tth`. Analyzer channels have separate outgoing directions. Published analyzer **Ψ** denotes rotation about its Bragg vector. [beamline.harima.riken.jp](http://beamline.harima.riken.jp) |


The HERIX distinction is substantial: its documented HRM uses crystal-pair Bragg angles of approximately **9.182°, 83.958°, and 7.821°** at 23.725 keV, while its analyzers operate near backscattering. Those are **historical optical design values**, not encoder readings or an assertion about every post-upgrade configuration. [journals.iucr.org](http://journals.iucr.org)

## 4. Proposed common sample-mounting paradigm

I would use the following as the common reference for TAVI and the comparison framework. **This is a proposed convention**, with measured mounting and instrument geometry supplying the actual transforms.


| Item                          | Common definition                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| ----------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Laboratory frame**          | **+z along the incident beam at the sample; +y vertically upward; +x completes the right-handed frame.** This follows the NeXus/McStas laboratory-frame convention. [nexus v2026.01 documentation](https://manual.nexusformat.org/design.html?utm_source=chatgpt.com)                                    |
| **Ideal mounting default**    | Choose two non-collinear reciprocal-space vectors **u** and **v**. At zero stage angles, put **Bu along +z** and **\\((Bu)\\times(Bv)\\) along +y**. This defines an ideal horizontal scattering-plane mount without selecting the same crystallographic plane for every sample.                         |
| **Actual crystal mounting**   | Store **\\(U\_0\\)** at physical stage zero. \\(B\\) maps \\(hkl\\) into a crystal Cartesian reciprocal frame; \\(U\_0\\) rotates that frame into the mount’s zero reference. Record whether \\(B\\) includes \\(2\\pi\\). [researchgate.net](http://researchgate.net)                                   |
| **Each physical stage**       | Store its **axis vector, parent/carrier stage, pivot, units and limits**, alongside the native name. NeXus `NXtransformations` provides an appropriate representation. [nexus v2026.01 documentation](https://manual.nexusformat.org/classes/base_classes/NXtransformations.html?utm_source=chatgpt.com) |
| **Readout conversion**        | Store sign, zero offset, relative-axis coupling, wrapping and selected branch. These convert a native reading into a physical rotation.                                                                                                                                                                  |
| **Sample surface and holder** | Record the surface normal, holder orientation and centering translations separately from the reciprocal-lattice orientation.                                                                                                                                                                             |
| **Analyzer bank**             | Give each channel its own outgoing-ray direction and energy definition. Keep sample scattering angles separate from internal optical Bragg angles.                                                                                                                                                       |


With column vectors, the common orientation calculation is

\\[ \\mathbf g\_{\\mathrm{lab}} = R\_{\\mathrm{outer}}(a_1)\\, R_2(a_2)\\cdots R\_{\\mathrm{inner}}(a_n)\\, U_0 B \\begin{pmatrix}h\\\\k\\\\l\\end{pmatrix}. \\]

The axes are expressed in their **parent frames**, so an outer rotation carries the inner axes with it. The momentum-transfer sign—such as \\(\\mathbf Q=\\mathbf k_i-\\mathbf k_f\\)—must also be declared and converted explicitly.

For TAVI, this gives a clear separation: **physical geometry and mounting internally; facility-specific labels and readout conversions at the interface**. It accommodates the verified TAVI and ID28 tilt sequences, the HERIX Eulerian cradle, and the partially documented BL43LXU mounting without assigning a universal meaning to ψ, φ, or χ.
